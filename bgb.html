Bailey and Goodman-Bacon's county changes, checked against this log

Bailey and Goodman-Bacon (2015, American Economic Review, "The War on Poverty's Experiment in Public Medicine") combined the counties that split or merged after 1959 so that each could be followed as one unit, and listed every combination in Tables A4 and A5 of their online appendix. We verify that list against this log: our extraction of Forstall (1996) is consistent with their process. Below we report their claims and whether each can be replicated from the data presented here. Of their 40 rows, 33 are verified directly. The remaining 7 are not county events: they are decisions to merge an independent city into the county around it, and two recodings, and nothing in Forstall or in the Census Bureau's list of county changes contradicts them.

Notes

  1. A row written City//County gives no year and no event. Under their rule a city that annexed county territory after 1959 is combined with that county; Forstall records the annexation in the city's note, and that note is the source cited.
  2. A decision rather than an event. For these independent cities they chose to merge the city into the county around it, with no boundary change after 1959 in Forstall or the Census Bureau's list behind it (three of the pairs are ones the SEER population files combine, which they also adopt); Columbus and Muscogee County were consolidated in 1971 with the county's code kept, and Ste. Genevieve County's code was renumbered. None of this is contradicted by the sources.
